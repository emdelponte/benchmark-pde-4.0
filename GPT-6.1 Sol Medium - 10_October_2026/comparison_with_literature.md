# Comparison with literature — searched after the independent analysis

The independent documents in outputs/independent_snapshot and the original analysis_plan.md were frozen before the first paper search. Record/README citations were visible earlier but their methods were not consulted. Literature resolved the outcome gate; analysis_plan_revisions.md was written before fitting. Thus the original predictor specifications are outcome-blind independent decisions, while the target repair and fitted results are explicitly **literature-informed**. A fully independent fitted-model comparison could not be completed under the supplied metadata.

The 2021 study independently documents strong weather-predictor correlation and the use of moisture/temperature proxies; these align with the audit's redundancy findings. It also explains that post-anthesis models can serve later grain-management objectives. Such predictors are unavailable for the present 00 UTC anthesis target. These comparisons concern different estimands and prediction times, not a replication failure. The source's Equation 1 was checked in publisher XML rather than guessed from a broken equation image.

## Additional insights and unresolved comparisons

The present audit contributes an explicit distinction between pre-labeled and prediction-time-eligible weather, finds universal CSV-versus-YAML day-length disagreement, quantifies shared weather profiles, and shows weak host-specific calibration despite near-zero pooled mean bias. The source-informed continuation tests a small fixed weather-only model under year/state grouping and temporal shift. Modest AUC and uncertain Brier gains suggest limited evidence for deployment under these restrictions. It does not demonstrate the absence of more useful raw-weather predictors.

The candidate set was curated in prior research, so new within-fold coefficient fitting cannot undo possible earlier outcome-informed feature engineering. Conversely, the fact that random resampling can split related weather profiles is a reason to question transportability, not a quantified estimate of optimism in the published numbers. No matched random-versus-grouped replication was performed; do not attribute the entire performance difference to dependence.

The 2024 article [Predicting plant disease epidemics using boosted regression trees](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2468042724000885) was also located as a related reproduction. It reports near-one AUCs for many models. Its validation was not sufficiently audited here, so those claims are not used as a trustworthy external benchmark or a basis for changing this model.

Access limitations: several DOI/publisher pages gave 403/fetch errors; the 2019 PMC page presented a browser challenge and Europe PMC XML returned HTTP 500. The comparison therefore explicitly limits claims to accessible primary text/abstracts. No undocumented exact coefficients, validation design or numeric performance were filled in for inaccessible portions.
