# Post-analysis comparison
The independent analysis and its limitations were written before looking up prior results. The threshold ambiguity can now be partly resolved. Shah et al. (2013) explicitly defined a major epidemic as severity ≥10%. Shah et al. (2021) reported 273 epidemics among 999 plots, exactly the count obtained here. Thus the supplementary endpoint recovers the published convention; the released Parquet and YAML still lack a binary field and explicit derivation. No model, cutoff or fitted result was changed after this search.

|Study and data|Objective, predictors and complexity|Validation and result|
|---|---|---|
|Shah et al. 2013; 527 observations, 31% events|Predict major epidemics with logistic regression. Considered 380 weather summaries of temperature, humidity and rainfall in 5–15-day pre- or post-flowering windows; subset search and bootstrap selection.|70% training, remaining observations for testing. Mean test misclassification was 19% lower relative to the then-operational models. Relative error reduction is a different quantity from our absolute Brier improvement.|
|Shah et al. 2014; earlier 527 observations|Boosted regression trees combined weather and agronomic characteristics. Mean RH, temperature and joint warm–humid hours were influential.|369 training and 158 test observations; test misclassification 0.23. This measures classification under a decision rule; our analysis estimated probabilities without a decision threshold.|
|Shah et al. 2021; 999 observations, 273 events|Compared 39 logistic base learners using 77 distinct weather summaries and explored ensemble gains. Individual models used one to three weather predictors, with agronomic predictors in the model family. Candidate information and timing varied across models.|Random tenfold cross-validation; individual-model Brier 0.160–0.183 (mean 0.171). Model predictions were correlated (mean pairwise r = 0.782).|
|Current analysis; 999 observations, 273 events|Two fixed weather summaries, each ending before flowering day; earlier mean temperature and low-VPD duration. No agronomic terms or tuned thresholds.|Year/state holdout Brier 0.193/0.196; chronological test 0.189. Mean risk exceeded observed prevalence in the chronological test by 5.2 percentage points.|

The studies agree that thermal and atmospheric moisture conditions carry predictive information. They do not establish that our particular VPD threshold or temperature window is optimal. Published model-specific risks incorporate information our weather-only task excludes. Row-level folds, grouped folds, post-anthesis windows and agronomic terms change the forecasting question. The difference between published and current Brier scores cannot be attributed to one of those choices without fitting both approaches on identical folds and information sets. Our additional contribution is quantifying geographic and chronological calibration loss under a conservative operational cutoff and showing how little certainty there is about improvement over prevalence.
