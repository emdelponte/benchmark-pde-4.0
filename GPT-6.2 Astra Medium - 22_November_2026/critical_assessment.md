# Independent assessment | recorded before literature comparison
The data support estimation of two weather slopes for a specified severity exceedance. They do not establish an operational epidemic forecast. Although both slopes stay positive when omitting years, the Brier gains are small relative to between-environment uncertainty. Calibration slopes under one indicate predictions are too dispersed in grouped validation. A near-zero pooled calibration offset is insufficient evidence of good calibration in a future state or year.

The moisture count is a proxy for sustained humid air; it cannot identify canopy wetness, inoculum abundance, infection timing or disease mechanism. Earlier temperature could encode seasonal, regional or crop-type differences. The windows overlap and estimates are observational. The reasonable coefficient precision conditional on years cannot resolve missing station and site identities or crossed dependence across years. The proxy weighting changes the estimand and may merge sites with coincident flowering. The disease index differs within identical apparent environments. Resistance class and residue may explain some variation; this weather-only prediction averages over the observed management and cultivar mix.

The audit found 273 threshold exceedances, 726 nonexceedances and only two slopes, with no tuned cutoffs or interactions. The effective geographic and temporal replication is much smaller than 999. Year folds train partly on future years; the forward split addresses this issue for one calendar boundary. Holding out a state tests geographic transfer at a coarse scale; nearby stations could straddle borders. Published feature engineering preceded this release, so freezing our own comparisons cannot remove all historical selection bias. Choosing the additive candidate used grouped results; those scores describe prespecified models and cannot independently validate the choice. The fixed-prediction bootstrap omits model refitting and crossed clustering; intervals are approximate. Random row splits would make repeated weather profiles available on both sides. No treatment benefit or prospective decision utility was estimated.

Most robust here are the small absolute accuracy gains, geographic variation and sensitivity of calibration to the evaluation population. The best timing, moisture threshold and biological effects remain exploratory. Values at the edge of the historical range have sparse support. The conservative day-zero exclusion may discard genuinely available near-flowering information. Station-hour timestamps and the aggregation code would settle that question. For a prospective test, freeze the endpoint, equation, issue time and station-availability rule, record independent sites and stations across several future seasons, and retain hourly completeness, actual flowering observations, cultivar resistance and management. Compare calibration and Brier score to an operational prevalence baseline and assess costs against a specified management decision before using probabilities to recommend intervention.
