## 1. Corpus of Published Studies Identified

Cross-referencing the Zenodo record (`10.5281/zenodo.23189372`, `FHBVer2` v2.1.0) and literature databases identified six primary peer-reviewed studies tracing the 20-year evolution of the U.S. Wheat & Barley Scab Initiative (USWBSI) forecasting dataset:

1. **De Wolf, Madden, & Lipps (2003)** — *Phytopathology* 93(4):428–435 (`10.1094/PHYTO.2003.93.4.428`). Foundational empirical risk assessment models on `N = 50` location-years across 4 states (`OH`, `ND`, `MO`, `KS`).
2. **Shah, Molineros, Paul, Willyerd, Madden, & De Wolf (2013)** — *Phytopathology* 103(9):906–919 (`10.1094/PHYTO-11-12-0304-R`). Expanded national matrix (`Version 1.0`, `N = 527` observations, 31% epidemics) evaluating ~380 pre- and post-anthesis weather summaries (`5, 7, 10, 14, 15 d`; `12H` and `24H`) plus host/agronomic factors (`resist`, `type`, `corn`).
3. **Shah, De Wolf, Paul, & Madden (2019a)** — *Phytopathology* 109(1):96–110 (`10.1094/PHYTO-11-17-0386-R`). Functional Data Analysis (FDA; penalized B-splines from `120 d` pre-anthesis to `20 d` post-anthesis) on `N = 865` observations across 16 states and 31 years, identifying multi-week weather divergences (`P`, `T`, `RH`, `VPD`, `TDD`) separating epidemic from non-epidemic curves.
4. **Shah, Paul, De Wolf, & Madden (2019b)** — *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B* 374(1775):20180273 (`10.1098/rstb.2018.0273`). Scalar-on-function logistic regression over continuous 140-day (`[-120, +20]`) weather series (`N = 865`).
5. **Shah, De Wolf, Paul, & Madden (2021)** — *PLOS Computational Biology* 17(3):e1008831 (`10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008831`). Analyzed the **exact `N = 999` `FHBVer2` dataset**, evaluating **38 logistic regression base models** (derived from window-pane, FDA, and lasso variable searches) and multi-model ensembles (unweighted soft-voting, Brier-weighted, and stacked lasso/ridge/elastic-net).
6. **Shah, De Wolf, Paul, & Madden (2023)** — *Phytopathology* 113(8):1483–1493 (`10.1094/PHYTO-10-22-0380-R`). *Into the Trees: Random Forests for Predicting Fusarium Head Blight Epidemics of Wheat in the United States*, applying Random Forests with wrapper feature selection (`Boruta`, `varSelRF`, `VSURF`) to `FHBVer2` (`N = 999`).

## 3. Detailed Synthesis: Agreements, Divergences, and Novel Insights

### 3.1 Independent Confirmations of Published Epidemiological Findings
Without consulting any published papers prior to locking our results, our audit and modeling converged on four central findings of the USWBSI modeling literature:
1. **Epidemic Threshold (`S >= 10%`):** Although `FHBVer2.parquet` omitted the binary indicator column, epidemiological reasoning around DON mycotoxin risk and multi-rater visual noise led us independently to the exact `S >= 10%` threshold used throughout `Shah et al. (2013, 2019a, 2019b, 2021, 2023)`.
2. **Weakness of Proximal Anthesis Temperature Alone:** `De Wolf et al. (2003)` noted that pre-anthesis models (`Model B`) were noticeably weaker than post-anthesis infection models (`Models A/I`), and `Shah et al. (2013, 2019a)` shifted away from `[-7, 0]` mean temperature toward VPD, dew point depression, and earlier thermal windows. Our independent models (`M0`, `M1`, `M3`, `M4`) quantitatively explained *why*: wheat flowering self-synchronizes with permissive `15–22°C` temperatures (`IQR = 15.8–21.4°C`), compressing thermal variance during `[-7, 0]` so that atmospheric moisture (`RH7`, `VPD.A.1`) dominates proximal risk.
3. **Two-Stage Pre-Anthesis Signal (`[-20, -10]` Warmth + `[-10, 0]` Moisture):** Our best-performing specification (`M2r: VPD.A.1 + T.A.2`) pairs 20-to-10-day pre-heading temperature (`T.A.2`) with 10-day pre-anthesis vapor pressure deficit (`VPD.A.1`). Both variables originate from the `VariablesMetaData.csv` window subset that `Shah et al. (2019a, 2021)` derived via Functional Data Analysis, confirming that pre-heading warmth (`[-20, -10]`, aligned with *Gibberella zeae* perithecial development on surface residue) adds genuine temporal predictive skill (`LOYO BSS` rising from `6.2%` in `RH7` alone to `7.8%` in `M2r`) beyond proximal humidity.
4. **High Pairwise Correlation Among Candidate Models:** `Shah et al. (2021)` emphasized that 38 distinct logistic regression specifications on `FHBVer2` produced predicted probabilities with an average pairwise correlation of `0.867`. Our sensitivity analysis `S1` independently observed the same phenomenon within pre-anthesis windows: swapping `7-d`, `10-d`, and `15-d` RH/VPD formulations shifted `LOYO AUC` by less than `0.011` (`0.648–0.659`), reflecting strong serial autocorrelation in synoptic moisture.

### 3.2 Novel Critical Insights & Limitations Exposed by Our Independent Analysis
Our audit and validation uncovered **five substantive methodological and data-quality issues** that qualify or challenge conclusions in the published `FHBVer2` literature:

1. **Discovery of the UTC `12H` ("Overnight") Physical Inversion Artifact:**
   - `Shah et al. (2013)` introduced `"overnight"` (`12H`, `20:00–08:00`) relative humidity, VPD, and temperature summaries alongside `24H` summaries, and `74` such `12H` variables remain in `FHBVer2` (`Shah et al. 2021, 2023`).
   - Cross-checking `FHBVer2DataDict.yaml` against empirical quantiles in [data_audit.md](file:///c:/Users/edelp/OneDrive/Documentos/GitHub/benchmark-pde-4.0/Gemini%20Carbon%20Medium%20-%2022_November_2026/data_audit.md) revealed that timestamps were left in **unadjusted UTC** (`20:00–08:00 UTC` = `15:00–03:00 CDT` / `16:00–04:00 EDT`). Consequently, `"overnight"` `12H` variables capture **peak mid-afternoon solar heating (`3–7 PM` local time)** and omit early-morning dew (`3–8 AM` local time), causing `"overnight"` RH to be *lower* than 24-hour RH in **79–86% of rows** and degrading out-of-sample LOYO skill (`S3`: `LOYO AUC` drops from `0.657` to `0.649`, `Cal_Slope` drops from `0.847` to `0.807`). Any biological interpretation of `12H` predictors in prior studies as "nocturnal moisture" is compromised by this timezone shift.
2. **Optimism of Plot-Level Random Cross-Validation Under Environmental Replication (`999` Plots vs. `545` Weather Profiles & `142` `state × year` Clusters):**
   - `Shah et al. (2021)` and `Shah et al. (2023)` evaluated multi-model ensembles and Random Forests using **10-fold observation-level cross-validation** across the `999` plot rows.
   - Our structural audit showed that `719` of the `999` plots share **exact identical weather vectors** (`265` multi-plot site-date profiles, up to 4 cultivars per trial), and `state × year` clusters exhibit `ICC = 0.717` (`DEFF = 12.39`). Under observation-level 10-fold CV, a Random Forest or flexible spline/ensemble trained on 3 cultivars from a given research station on a given flowering date predicts the 4th cultivar at that *exact same weather profile* in the held-out fold!
   - Our controlled comparison in [results.md](file:///c:/Users/edelp/OneDrive/Documentos/GitHub/benchmark-pde-4.0/Gemini%20Carbon%20Medium%20-%2022_November_2026/results.md) proved that random 10-fold row CV inflates flexible learners (`E2_GAM15d` leaps from `LOSO AUC = 0.594` / `LOYO = 0.648` to `Random10x5 = 0.696`; `M5a_L1Top3` leaps from `0.619` / `0.640` to `0.695`). Thus, the apparent superiority of flexible Random Forests (`Shah et al. 2023`) or high-dimensional variable sets over 2-parameter logistic regression partly reflects **within-environment memorization** rather than superior prospective generalization to unseen years or states.
3. **Selection-Bias Leakage When Screening FDA / Window Predictors on the Full Archive:**
   - In `Shah et al. (2021)`, several of the 38 base logistic models incorporated weather windows (`P.A.*`, `T.14T22.*`, `TDD.*`, `VPD.A.*`) whose exact start and end days (`e.g., [-102, -94]`, `[-72, -62]`) had previously been visually or statistically identified from functional smoothed curves across `865` overlapping archive observations (`Shah et al. 2019a`). Re-evaluating those pre-screened windows via 10-fold CV on `FHBVer2` (`N = 999`, which embeds those 865 rows) understates variable-selection optimism. When we forced `M5a` and `E1` (`LASSO-174`) to repeat variable screening *strictly inside* grouped training folds, data-driven selection underperformed domain-fixed 2-parameter `M2r` (`LOYO AUC = 0.640` and `0.556` vs. `0.671`).
4. **Disentangling Host Resistance (`resist`) + Post-Anthesis Weather from Pre-Anthesis Weather Skill:**
   - Published Brier scores (`0.160–0.183` in `Shah et al. 2021`) and AUCs (`0.79–0.84` in `Shah et al. 2019b`) bundle three distinct information sources: pre-anthesis weather, host resistance (`resist`), and post-anthesis weather (`[0, +10]` to `[0, +20]`). Our Sensitivity `S4` decomposed this ceiling under strict `LOYO` validation: pre-anthesis weather alone achieves `AUC = 0.671, Brier = 0.185` (`BSS = +7.8%`); adding `resist + corn` jumps `LOYO AUC` to **`0.723` (`Brier = 0.173`, `BSS = +13.6%`)**; the remaining gap to `Brier ~ 0.160` reflects post-anthesis infection-window moisture (`0` to `+10 d`) and within-cluster CV sharing.
5. **Spring-Wheat Regional Failure Mode & Metadata Discrepancies:**
   - Our subpopulation audit (`S5`) uncovered a critical operational blind spot rarely foregrounded in pooled national metrics: pure pre-anthesis weather models lose virtually all discriminative and Brier skill inside **Northern Plains spring wheat** (`n = 265`, `LOYO AUC = 0.568, BSS = -2.6%`), where epidemic risk is overwhelmingly driven by summer post-anthesis storms and cultivar susceptibility rather than pre-flowering humidity.
   - Finally, our cross-check documented concrete archival discrepancies in Zenodo `23189372` (`location` omitted from `.parquet`/`.yaml` despite `README.txt`; `win_length` off by `-1` day across all `329` rows of `VariablesMetaData.csv`; unflagged exact complements `T157 + T.L15.PRE7.24H = 192`) that future users of this benchmark dataset should account for.

  - Choice of `S >= 10%` binary epidemic definition (`273` events), cluster-adjusted effective sample size ($E_{\text{eff,sy}} = 22$), and `LOYO` / `LOSO` / `5×10` grouped `state × year` CV design.
  - All model specifications (`M0`–`M5`, `M0r`, `M2r`, `E1`, `E2`), parameter estimates, cluster-robust standard errors, calibration curves, random-vs-grouped CV leakage demonstration, `resist + corn` ceiling check, and spring-wheat transportability failure diagnosis.
- **Obtained AFTER consulting the literature** (this document only):
  - Identification of the specific origins of the variable-naming strata in `FHBVer2` (`De Wolf 2003` legacy codes vs. `Shah 2013` window codes vs. `Shah 2019a/2021` FDA/ensemble codes).
  - Confirmation that published `FHBVer2` ensemble/RF benchmarks (`Shah et al. 2021, 2023`) included `resist` + post-anthesis windows and relied on observation-level 10-fold CV, quantitatively explaining both the concordance of our Brier scores (`0.185` weather-only; `0.173` with `resist + corn`) with `Shah et al. (2021)` (`0.160–0.183`) and why complex learners appeared stronger in published observation-level CV than under prospective grouped validation.
