## 1. Epidemiological Plausibility vs. Observational Confounding of Selected Predictors

### What Is Biologically Plausible (Robust Evidence)
* **Proximal atmospheric moisture (`VPD.A.1` `[-10, 0]` in `M2`/`M2r`, `RH7` `[-7, 0]` in `M0`/`M0r`):** Across every model specification, validation split, and threshold (`5%`, `10%`, `15%`), pre-anthesis moisture availability during the 7–10 days immediately preceding flowering is the single most robust meteorological predictor ($p \le 0.0001$ even under 142-cluster `state × year` sandwich SEs). Biologically, *Fusarium graminearum* perithecia on surface crop residue require hydration and low atmospheric evaporative demand (`low VPD` / `high RH`) to mature and forcibly discharge ascospores onto emerging wheat spikes, and synoptically, low 10-d pre-anthesis VPD reflects wet soil and humid air masses that persist into early anthesis.
* **Warm pre-heading thermal background (`T.A.2`, `[-20, -10]`):** While ambient air temperatures during the final week before anthesis (`T.A.PRE7.24H`, median `18.5°C`) are already non-limiting because wheat phenology self-aligns anthesis with moderate late-spring/early-summer warmth, temperatures 10–20 days earlier (`T.A.2`, 1st quartile `14.6°C`, min `9.8°C`) frequently dip below the ~15°C thermal threshold below which *F. graminearum* perithecial initiation slows markedly. Moreover, `T.A.2` remained positively associated within winter wheat alone (`n = 734`), ruling out a simple winter-vs.-spring market-class artifact.

### Where Causal Interpretation Must Be Resisted (Speculation vs. Evidence)
* **Synoptic & geographic confounding of `T.A.2` and `VPD.A.1`:** Even within winter wheat, warmer `[-20, -10]` temperatures and lower `[-10, 0]` VPD partially track humid lower-Midwest / Mid-Atlantic climates (`MO, MD, IN, KY`) where no-till maize–wheat rotations and historically higher background inoculum prevail, relative to cooler or semi-arid Plains states (`KS, NE, ND, SD`). Thus, $\hat{\beta}_{\text{T.A.2}} = +0.151$ and $\hat{\beta}_{\text{VPD.A.1}} = -2.834$ capture **combined microclimatic suitability + regional agroecological risk**, not pure controlled chamber dose–response curves.
* **Psychrometric coupling between `VPD` and `T`:** Because $\text{VPD} = e_s(T)(1 - \text{RH}/100)$, holding `VPD.A.1` constant while increasing temperature mathematically implies higher absolute water vapor pressure (higher dewpoint). Analysts must not interpret the `T.A.2` coefficient in isolation from `VPD.A.1`.

## 2. Sample Size, Effective Degrees of Freedom, and Overfitting Assessment

* **Why naive Events-Per-Variable (EPV) is dangerously misleading here:** A mechanical calculation ($E = 273$ events / $p = 3$ parameters $\approx 91$ EPV) would suggest the dataset could comfortably support 15–25 parameters. Our variance-component audit refuted that assumption: because co-located plots share identical weather (`545` profiles, $\text{ICC} = 0.824$) and cluster tightly within `142` `state × year` environments ($\text{ICC} = 0.717$, $\text{DEFF} = 12.39$), the dataset contains only **$E_{\text{eff,sy}} \approx 22$ effective independent state-year epidemic events** (and $E_{\text{eff,env}} \approx 121$ weather-environment events).
* **Empirical confirmation of overfitting beyond 2–3 fixed terms:**
  * Even when restricted to $\le 3$ predictors with unsupervised collinearity pruning ($|r| < 0.85$) nested strictly inside training folds, data-driven LASSO variable selection (`M5a`) produced an illusory apparent AUC gain (`0.716` vs. `0.671–0.690`) that **vanished completely out-of-sample** (`LOYO AUC = 0.651`, `Cal_Slope = 0.660`; `LOSO Brier = 0.194`, `Cal_Slope = 0.608`). That `~0.34–0.39` calibration-slope shrinkage is classic selection-induced optimism ("winner's curse" across 30 candidate predictors).
  * Unconstrained 174-predictor LASSO (`E1`, `LOYO AUC = 0.447`) and 2D penalized splines (`E2_GAM15d`, `LOYO Cal_Slope = 0.445`, `LOSO Cal_Slope = 0.370`) degraded performance below a 1-parameter `RH7` model!
  * Conversely, our final 2-parameter model `M2r` (~11 effective state-year events per parameter) and 1-parameter fallback `M0r` (~22 effective state-year events per parameter) achieved out-of-year calibration slopes near `0.89–0.90`, indicating minimal overfitting.
* **Residual post-selection caveat on `M2r`:** Because `M2r` (`VPD.A.1 + T.A.2`) was obtained by dropping the non-significant `TRH` term ($p = 0.73$) from pre-specified `M2` after inspecting full-sample inference, its apparent parsimony gain over `M2` (`LOYO AUC 0.671` vs. `0.666`; `BSS 7.8%` vs. `6.8%`) carries ~1 degree of freedom of post-hoc simplification choice. Importantly, even if judged strictly by the untouched pre-specified `M2` (`LOYO AUC = 0.666, BSS = 6.8%`) or baseline `M0` (`LOYO AUC = 0.649, BSS = 5.6%`), all substantive conclusions are identical.

## 3. Sensitivity to Exposure Windows, Predictor Redundancy, and Metadata Artifacts

1. **Predictor substitutability ("Rashomon effect"):** Pre-anthesis moisture variables (`RH7`, `RH.A.PRE10.24H`, `RH.A.PRE15.24H`, `VPD.A.1`, `TDD.A.1`, `RH.G80.CHD.1`) are strongly intercorrelated ($|r| = 0.78\text{–}0.91$). Consequently, `M0r` (`RH7`), `M0` (`T.A.PRE7 + RH7`), `M2` (`TRH15 + VPD10 + T20_10`), and `M2r` (`VPD10 + T20_10`) all perform within a narrow band (`LOYO AUC = 0.649–0.671`, `Brier = 0.185–0.189`), with overlapping cluster-bootstrap 95% confidence intervals (`~[0.57, 0.74]`). We can conclude robustly that **7–10 day pre-anthesis atmospheric moisture + pre-heading warmth** predict risk, NOT that `VPD.A.1` is uniquely causal over `RH7` or `TDD.A.1`.
2. **Continuous vs. threshold-count predictors:** Single-variable hourly threshold counts (`TRH807`, `TRH.15T30nRHG80.PRE15.24H`) consistently underperformed continuous means (`RH7`, `VPD.A.1`) by `0.07–0.09` AUC units (`0.567–0.584` vs. `0.649–0.671`). Dichotomizing hourly series at rigid cutoffs (`RH >= 80%`, `T >= 15°C`) throws away information on whether `"sub-threshold"` hours were moderately humid (`78% RH`) vs. desiccating (`35% RH`).
3. **Dataset-specific UTC and day-0 operational caveats:**
   * Our audit proved that the `60` pre-anthesis `"12H overnight"` variables (`20:00–08:00 UTC`) actually measure mid-afternoon-to-early-night (`15:00–03:00 CDT`) in North America, inverting expected day–night humidity relationships and slightly degrading temporal validation (`S3`). Future dataset releases should convert hourly station timestamps to local solar time before computing nocturnal wetness proxies.
   * Because `window_end_day == 0` includes `00:00–23:00 UTC` on the flowering date (`19:00 CDT` on `fdate - 1` through `18:00 CDT` on `fdate`), `M2r`/`M0` require weather through late afternoon of the anthesis date (or short-range numerical weather forecasts for day `0` if spray decisions are made early that morning). Truncating weather 5 days earlier (`<= -5 d`) drops `LOYO AUC` from `0.671` to `0.616`.

## 4. Structural Ceiling, Subpopulation Failures, and Transportability Limits

The most important scientific conclusion of this benchmark is **why pure pre-anthesis weather models hit a ceiling around `AUC = 0.66–0.67` (`BSS = 7–8%`)** and exhibit severe environment-to-environment variability:

1. **Omitted agronomic susceptibility and local inoculum (`20%` within-trial discordance):** In `20.0%` (`53/265`) of multi-plot trials with *identical* weather, susceptible (`VS`/`S`) or corn-residue plots suffered epidemics (`S >= 10%`) while moderately resistant (`MR`) or no-corn plots right beside them did not! Adding just `resist + corn` alongside `M2` weather increased `LOYO AUC` from `0.666` to `0.723` and **doubled Brier Skill Score (`6.8%` to `13.6%`)**. A pure weather model outputs an *average-cultivar* probability that systematically under-predicts risk for `VS` wheat (`46.7%` observed rate) and over-predicts risk for `MR` wheat (`13.0%` observed rate).
2. **Failure in Northern Plains Spring Wheat (`ND, SD, MN`):** When evaluated solely within spring wheat (`n = 265`), `M1` and `M2` drop to `LOYO AUC = 0.568–0.572` (`BSS = -2.6%`). Spring wheat flowers in late June–July under convective continental regimes where pre-anthesis VPD is less coupled to post-anthesis spike-wetness duration and where macro-regional calibration from Eastern winter wheat does not transfer cleanly.
3. **Unobserved post-anthesis infection weather and missing precipitation/wetness sensors:** Ascospore infection of extruded anthers and spike colonization occur *during and 5–14 days after* anthesis. Pre-anthesis weather cannot foresee whether a dry pre-anthesis spell will be broken by rain at flowering—or whether a humid pre-anthesis period will be followed by a hot, dry post-anthesis week that arrests symptom development. Moreover, `FHBVer2` contains only temperature, dewpoint, RH, VPD, TDD, and pressure from nearest standard/citizen stations—**no rainfall intensity/frequency, canopy leaf/spike wetness duration, wind/turbulence, irrigation status, or distance/elevation offset between plot and weather station** (`location` coordinates were omitted from the release, and even `location` names were omitted from `.parquet`!).
4. **Geographic miscalibration in unmonitored states (`LOSO`):** While pooled `LOSO` calibration-in-the-large looks deceptively near zero (`Cal_Int = -0.030`), state-level breakdown ([fig3_loso_state_transportability.png](file:///c:/Users/edelp/OneDrive/Documentos/GitHub/benchmark-pde-4.0/Gemini%20Carbon%20Medium%20-%2022_November_2026/output/figures/fig3_loso_state_transportability.png)) exposes large regional offsets: `MO` (`63.3%` observed vs. `24.7%` predicted) and `IN` (`43.2%` vs. `24.5%`) are under-predicted, whereas `WI, NY, DE, AR` (`0%` observed vs. `17–38%` predicted) and `PA, MN, MI` (`4–9%` observed vs. `20–29%` predicted) experience substantial false-positive risk predictions—likely reflecting trial-selection bias in cooperator nurseries (e.g., whether cooperators sited trials in river bottoms or misted/high-residue hotspots vs. commercial-like dryland fields).

## 5. Requirements for Prospective Validation and Operational Deployment

Before deploying an anthesis-time FHB risk model prospectively:
1. **Integrate host resistance (`resist`) and residue/cropping history (`corn`) explicitly** as baseline risk modifiers alongside the 2-term weather core (`VPD.A.1 + T.A.2` or `RH7`), calibrated separately or hierarchically for **winter wheat** vs. **spring wheat**.
2. **Collect prospective, multi-year commercial/sentinel field cohorts (post-2015)** with exact GPS coordinates, station distance/quality metadata, local-timezone hourly precipitation and canopy wetness, and quantitative **DON mycotoxin (ppm)** alongside visual FHB index (`S`), avoiding retrospective cooperator-nursery selection bias.
3. **Incorporate short-range (3–7 day) numerical weather forecasts at anthesis** so predicted post-flowering infection moisture (`[0, +7 d]`) can be combined legally at decision time with observed pre-anthesis inoculum-maturation weather (`[-20, 0]`).
4. **Perform regional recalibration (updating the intercept $\alpha_r$)** to local baseline prevalence before issuing spray advisories in new states, evaluating clinical/agronomic net benefit (decision-curve analysis weighing fungicide cost ~\$15–\$25/ha against yield/DON dockage losses) rather than relying on arbitrary $p = 0.50$ cutoffs.
